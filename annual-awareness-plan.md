# Annual Security Awareness Plan

## Purpose

This Annual Security Awareness Plan provides a structured 12-month approach for delivering continuous, role-based, and measurable security awareness across the organization.

The plan is designed to help organizations move beyond once-a-year compliance training and build practical security behaviours across employees, contractors, managers, executives, and high-risk teams.

## Objectives

The objectives of the annual awareness plan are to:

1. Improve employee understanding of cyber threats and safe working practices.
2. Reinforce secure behaviours through regular communication and practical activities.
3. Reduce human-related cyber risks such as phishing, credential compromise, data leakage, fraud, and unsafe AI use.
4. Support compliance with internal policies, privacy requirements, and security frameworks.
5. Build a stronger reporting culture.
6. Provide role-based training for high-risk teams.
7. Measure awareness effectiveness and improve the program over time.

## Scope

This plan applies to:

- Employees
- Contractors
- Temporary staff
- Executives
- Managers
- IT and privileged users
- Finance teams
- HR teams
- Developers and technical teams
- Other high-risk business functions

## Annual Awareness Approach

The program should combine the following activities:

- Onboarding awareness for new starters
- Annual mandatory training for all staff
- Monthly awareness campaigns
- Role-based training
- Phishing and social engineering awareness
- AI-era threat awareness
- Practical exercises and tabletop scenarios
- Security reporting reminders
- Metrics review and continuous improvement

## 12-Month Awareness Plan

| Month | Theme | Target Audience | Key Activities | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | Cybersecurity Basics | All staff | Launch annual awareness plan, remind staff of security responsibilities, promote MFA and password hygiene | Staff understand the year’s awareness priorities and basic security expectations |
| February | Phishing and Social Engineering | All staff | Share phishing examples, explain reporting process, run short quiz or awareness post | Improved phishing recognition and reporting |
| March | Data Protection and Privacy | All staff, HR, managers | Reinforce data handling, secure sharing, clean desk, privacy and confidentiality requirements | Reduced risk of accidental data exposure |
| April | Passwords, MFA and Account Security | All staff, IT support | Promote strong authentication, MFA fatigue awareness, password manager guidance | Improved account protection behaviours |
| May | Secure Remote and Hybrid Working | Remote and hybrid staff | Share home working checklist, Wi-Fi safety, device locking and safe collaboration guidance | Improved remote working security |
| June | Ransomware Awareness | All staff, IT, managers | Explain ransomware entry points, suspicious attachments, backup importance and early reporting | Better ransomware prevention and early escalation |
| July | Travel and Mobile Security | Executives, mobile workers, all staff | Share travel security checklist, public Wi-Fi risks, lost device reporting guidance | Reduced mobile and travel-related security risk |
| August | Cloud and Collaboration Tools | All staff, technical teams | Promote safe file sharing, link permissions, approved collaboration platforms and data classification | Safer use of shared digital workspaces |
| September | Insider Risk and Workplace Security | All staff, managers, HR | Explain respectful reporting, access misuse, physical security and policy expectations | Improved awareness of insider and workplace risks |
| October | Cybersecurity Awareness Month | All staff | Run organization-wide campaign, leadership message, quiz, posters, events and reporting reminder | Increased engagement and awareness visibility |
| November | Fraud and Payment Security | Finance, procurement, executives | Run payment fraud awareness, supplier impersonation, callback verification and deepfake fraud guidance | Reduced risk of payment redirection and invoice fraud |
| December | Year-End Security Review | All staff, leadership | Review lessons learned, publish key metrics, share holiday scam reminders and next-year priorities | Improved reflection, reporting and planning for the next cycle |

## Quarterly Delivery Plan

## Quarter 1: Foundation and Baseline

### Focus

Establish security awareness priorities, complete baseline training, and reinforce essential cyber hygiene.

### Activities

- Launch the annual awareness plan.
- Confirm program owner and executive sponsor.
- Deliver annual core awareness training.
- Share security reporting routes.
- Run phishing and social engineering awareness campaign.
- Review completion rates and early engagement metrics.

### Deliverables

- Annual security awareness plan
- Training completion report
- Initial awareness communications
- Phishing awareness materials
- Baseline metrics

## Quarter 2: Role-Based and Behaviour-Focused Awareness

### Focus

Deliver targeted awareness for high-risk teams and reinforce practical behaviours.

### Activities

- Deliver finance fraud awareness training.
- Deliver HR data protection awareness.
- Deliver privileged user awareness.
- Run secure remote working campaign.
- Reinforce password, MFA and account security.
- Collect feedback from teams.

### Deliverables

- Role-based training records
- Finance fraud checklist
- HR data handling reminder
- Privileged access awareness material
- Updated risk observations

## Quarter 3: Practical Exercises and AI-Era Risks

### Focus

Test awareness, reinforce reporting behaviours, and address AI-enabled threats.

### Activities

- Run phishing reporting drill.
- Deliver AI-era awareness communication.
- Cover deepfake, voice cloning and AI-assisted social engineering.
- Run lost device or data handling scenario.
- Deliver developer or technical AI security awareness where relevant.
- Review simulation and reporting metrics.

### Deliverables

- Phishing simulation report
- AI-era awareness guidance
- Exercise lessons learned
- Improvement action log
- Updated training content

## Quarter 4: Review, Improvement and Planning

### Focus

Review program effectiveness, report outcomes, and prepare the next annual cycle.

### Activities

- Run fraud and payment security campaign.
- Review training completion and reporting trends.
- Analyse human-related incident patterns.
- Gather feedback from managers and staff.
- Report awareness metrics to leadership.
- Update the annual plan for the next year.

### Deliverables

- Annual awareness report
- Metrics dashboard
- Lessons learned register
- Next-year awareness plan
- Updated campaign calendar

## AI-Era Awareness Priorities

The annual plan should include awareness on AI-enabled threats and safe AI use.

## AI-Enabled Threats to Cover

- AI-generated phishing emails
- Deepfake audio and video
- Voice cloning
- Executive impersonation
- Supplier impersonation
- AI-assisted business email compromise
- Fake meeting invitations
- Requests for MFA codes or account recovery
- Polished social engineering messages with no spelling errors

## Safe AI Use Topics

Employees should understand:

- Which AI tools are approved for work use
- What information must not be entered into public AI tools
- Why confidential, personal, customer, security, legal or financial data must be protected
- Why AI outputs require human review
- How to report suspected AI misuse
- How to escalate uncertainty before using AI for sensitive work

## Communication Plan

The awareness program should use multiple communication channels.

Recommended channels include:

- Email
- Intranet
- Microsoft Teams or Slack
- Posters or digital signage
- Team meetings
- Short videos
- Leadership messages
- Security champions
- Quizzes and scenario-based activities

## Monthly Campaign Template

Each monthly campaign should define:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Campaign Theme | The topic for the month |
| Target Audience | Who the campaign is for |
| Key Message | The main behaviour to reinforce |
| Delivery Channel | Email, intranet, Teams, posters, briefing |
| Call to Action | What employees should do |
| Owner | Person or team responsible |
| Launch Date | Date campaign begins |
| Success Measure | How effectiveness will be measured |

## Awareness Metrics

The following metrics should be reviewed throughout the year.

## Participation Metrics

- Training completion rate
- Completion by department
- New starter completion rate
- Role-based training attendance
- Late completion rate

## Behaviour Metrics

- Suspicious email reporting rate
- Time to first phishing report
- Number of security questions raised
- Quality of reported suspicious activity
- Repeat susceptibility trends in phishing simulations

## Risk Metrics

- Number of incidents linked to human error
- Number of data handling incidents
- Number of successful phishing incidents
- Number of payment fraud attempts
- Number of unapproved AI tool usage incidents

## Culture Metrics

- Employee confidence in reporting
- Staff feedback after training
- Manager participation
- Security champion engagement
- Leadership visibility in security messaging

## Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Executive Sponsor | Supports the program and reinforces security culture |
| Security Team | Owns awareness content, campaigns, metrics and improvement actions |
| GRC / Compliance | Aligns the plan with policies, risks and regulatory expectations |
| HR / People Team | Supports onboarding, training assignment and records |
| Department Managers | Encourage participation and reinforce team-level behaviours |
| Security Champions | Share security messages and provide feedback from teams |
| Employees and Contractors | Complete training and report suspicious activity |

## Success Criteria

The annual awareness plan is successful when:

- Training completion targets are met.
- Reporting of suspicious activity improves.
- Staff know how and when to report incidents.
- High-risk teams complete role-based training.
- Awareness content is updated based on threat trends.
- AI-era risks are included in training and communications.
- Leadership supports and reinforces the program.
- Lessons learned are used to improve controls and behaviours.

## Example Annual Targets

Organizations may set targets such as:

| Target | Example Measure |
|---|---|
| Core training completion | 95% completion by due date |
| New starter training | 100% completion within 10 working days |
| Role-based training | 90% completion for high-risk teams |
| Reporting improvement | 20% increase in suspicious email reports |
| Phishing simulation improvement | Reduced repeat susceptibility over time |
| AI acceptable use training | 95% completion for all staff |
| Executive participation | Annual executive briefing completed |

## Continuous Improvement

At the end of the year, the organization should review:

1. What worked well.
2. What did not work.
3. Which teams need additional support.
4. Which risks increased or decreased.
5. Which incidents were linked to behaviour or awareness gaps.
6. Whether reporting improved.
7. Whether AI-related risks need stronger controls.
8. What should be changed in the next annual plan.

## Disclaimer

This annual awareness plan is a practical template and should be adapted to the organization’s size, sector, workforce, regulatory obligations, threat profile and risk appetite.
