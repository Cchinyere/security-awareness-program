# HR Team Security Awareness

## Purpose

HR teams handle highly sensitive employee, candidate, payroll, identity, and personal data. This makes HR a common target for phishing, payroll fraud, impersonation, document-based malware, and social engineering.

This training helps HR staff protect personal information, verify sensitive requests, and respond appropriately to suspicious activity.

## Key Risks

HR teams are exposed to risks such as:

- Payroll fraud
- Employee impersonation
- Recruitment scams
- Malicious CVs or attachments
- Personal data leakage
- Unauthorized access to employee records
- Social engineering by email, phone, or messaging platforms
- Requests to change bank details or personal information
- Accidental sharing of confidential HR documents

## Required Behaviours

HR staff should:

1. Verify requests to change payroll, bank, address, or personal information.
2. Use approved HR systems and secure storage locations.
3. Avoid sending sensitive employee data through insecure channels.
4. Be cautious with attachments from unknown candidates or external parties.
5. Confirm unusual requests through trusted contact methods.
6. Report suspicious activity immediately.
7. Apply data minimization by only sharing what is necessary.
8. Follow retention and disposal requirements for HR records.

## AI-Era Considerations

Attackers may now use AI to create more convincing HR-related scams, including:

- AI-generated job applications
- Fake candidate profiles
- Deepfake interview attempts
- Voice-cloned employee requests
- Polished phishing messages with fewer spelling errors
- Fake legal, payroll, or benefits-related requests
- Synthetic identity documents

HR teams should not rely only on writing style, voice, or apparent professionalism as proof of authenticity.

## High-Risk HR Requests

Extra verification should be required for:

- Payroll bank detail changes
- Requests for employee records
- Requests from senior leaders asking for employee data
- Requests involving disciplinary, legal, or medical information
- Urgent requests to bypass HR processes
- External requests for employment verification
- Requests to reset employee access or credentials

## Verification Protocol

Before acting on sensitive HR requests:

1. Stop and assess whether the request is unusual, urgent, sensitive, or outside normal process.
2. Verify identity using trusted contact details already held by the organization.
3. Do not use phone numbers or email addresses supplied in the suspicious request.
4. Require approval where policy requires it.
5. Record verification steps where appropriate.
6. Escalate suspicious requests to HR leadership, security, or compliance.

## Data Protection Expectations

HR staff should:

- Handle personal data lawfully and securely.
- Share employee data only with authorized recipients.
- Use encryption or secure portals where required.
- Avoid storing HR records on personal devices.
- Avoid copying sensitive information into unapproved AI tools.
- Report accidental data sharing quickly.
- Follow internal retention and deletion requirements.

## Scenario Examples

### Scenario 1: Payroll Change Request

An employee emails HR asking to change their bank details urgently before payday.

Expected response:

- Do not process immediately based only on the email.
- Verify the request through the approved HR or payroll process.
- Contact the employee using trusted details already on file.
- Record the verification if required.

### Scenario 2: Suspicious CV Attachment

A recruiter receives a CV attachment from an unknown applicant.

Expected response:

- Open attachments only through approved systems.
- Be cautious of unexpected file types.
- Do not enable macros.
- Report suspicious files to IT or security.

### Scenario 3: Executive Request for Employee Data

A senior leader appears to request a full employee list urgently.

Expected response:

- Verify the request through a trusted internal channel.
- Confirm whether the requester is authorized to receive the data.
- Share only the minimum required information.
- Escalate if the request feels unusual or pressured.

## Training Topics

HR security awareness training should cover:

- Personal data protection
- Payroll fraud prevention
- Recruitment-related cyber risks
- Malicious attachments
- Social engineering
- Secure document handling
- Identity verification
- AI-generated scams and deepfakes
- Incident reporting
- Approved AI tool use

## Metrics

Recommended metrics include:

- HR training completion rate
- Number of suspicious HR-related requests reported
- Number of payroll change requests verified correctly
- Number of HR data handling incidents
- Time taken to report suspicious activity
- Completion of AI acceptable use training by HR staff

## Key Takeaways

- HR data is highly sensitive and valuable to attackers.
- Urgent requests should not bypass verification.
- AI can make scams look more professional and realistic.
- Personal data should never be entered into unapproved AI tools.
- Early reporting reduces harm.
