# Phishing Reporting Drill

## Purpose

This exercise helps organizations test whether employees can identify and report suspicious emails, messages, or links quickly and correctly.

The purpose is not to catch people out. It is to improve awareness, encourage early reporting, and identify where additional support or controls may be needed.

## Objectives

The objectives of this exercise are to:

1. Improve phishing recognition.
2. Test whether employees know how to report suspicious messages.
3. Measure reporting speed and quality.
4. Reinforce a no-blame security culture.
5. Identify teams that may need additional support.
6. Improve security awareness content based on real behaviour.

## Target Audience

This exercise can be used for:

- All staff
- New starters
- Finance teams
- HR teams
- Customer support teams
- Executives
- Contractors
- High-risk departments

## Scenario Overview

Employees receive a simulated suspicious email or message. The message should contain signs of phishing, such as urgency, an unusual sender, a suspicious link, or a request to take action outside normal process.

The employee is expected to report the message using the approved reporting channel.

## Example Scenario

An employee receives an email that appears to come from IT support.

The message says:

> Your account storage is almost full. Click the link below to avoid losing access to your mailbox.

The message includes a link to a fake login page or safe simulation landing page.

## Key Learning Points

Employees should learn to check:

- Sender address
- Link destination
- Unexpected urgency
- Poorly explained requests
- Requests for login details
- Attachments from unknown sources
- Requests that bypass normal process
- Messages asking for MFA codes or password resets

## AI-Era Considerations

Modern phishing emails may be well-written and professional because attackers can use AI tools to generate convincing messages.

Employees should understand that:

- Poor spelling is no longer a reliable phishing indicator.
- AI can create personalized phishing messages.
- Attackers may use real job titles, supplier names, or business context.
- Deepfake audio or voice-cloned calls may support phishing attempts.
- Suspicious requests should be verified through trusted channels.

## Exercise Rules

The exercise should be:

- Approved before launch
- Ethical and proportionate
- Educational rather than punitive
- Designed without public shaming
- Followed by learning feedback

## How to Run the Exercise

1. Define the objective of the drill.
2. Select the target audience.
3. Create a realistic but ethical phishing scenario.
4. Confirm the reporting channel is working.
5. Launch the simulation.
6. Monitor reports and responses.
7. Record key metrics.
8. Send follow-up learning communication.
9. Review lessons learned.
10. Update awareness content where needed.

## Metrics to Track

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| Reporting Rate | Percentage of users who reported the message |
| Click Rate | Percentage of users who clicked the link |
| Time to First Report | How quickly the first user reported the message |
| Quality of Reports | Whether reports included useful information |
| Repeat Susceptibility | Users or teams repeatedly interacting with simulations |
| Follow-Up Completion | Completion of any refresher training assigned |

## Ethical Requirements

Do not:

- Publicly name employees who clicked.
- Shame or embarrass individuals.
- Use distressing themes such as job loss, health scares, or personal emergencies.
- Collect real passwords.
- Use the exercise as a disciplinary trap.
- Ignore positive reporting behaviour.

Do:

- Thank employees who reported.
- Provide immediate learning.
- Share aggregated results.
- Focus on improvement.
- Use results to strengthen both people and controls.

## Post-Exercise Communication

Use a message like this:

> Thank you to everyone who reported the recent phishing simulation. This exercise was designed to help us strengthen our ability to recognise and report suspicious messages. If you clicked the message, please do not feel embarrassed. The most important action is to report quickly so the security team can respond and reduce impact.

## Success Criteria

The exercise is successful when:

- Employees understand how to report suspicious messages.
- Reporting rates improve over time.
- Staff feel safe reporting mistakes.
- Lessons learned are captured.
- Awareness content is improved based on results.
- Technical and process gaps are identified where relevant.

## Disclaimer

This exercise should be adapted to the organization’s policies, workforce, culture, legal requirements, and risk appetite.
