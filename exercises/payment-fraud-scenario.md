# Payment Fraud Scenario

## Purpose

This exercise helps organizations test whether employees can identify, verify, and escalate suspicious payment requests.

Payment fraud can involve invoice manipulation, supplier impersonation, executive impersonation, business email compromise, deepfake voice requests, and changes to bank details.

## Objectives

The objectives are to:

1. Improve awareness of payment fraud techniques.
2. Reinforce verification before payment changes.
3. Test escalation procedures.
4. Reduce risk of business email compromise.
5. Improve coordination between finance, procurement, leadership, and security.
6. Address AI-enabled fraud risks such as voice cloning and deepfakes.

## Target Audience

This exercise is suitable for:

- Finance teams
- Procurement teams
- Accounts payable
- Executives
- Department managers
- Supplier relationship managers
- Customer support teams where payment information is handled

## Scenario Overview

A finance employee receives an urgent email that appears to come from a known supplier. The supplier says their bank details have changed and asks for the next invoice payment to be sent to the new account.

Shortly after, the employee receives a phone call from someone claiming to be the supplier’s account manager, pressuring them to process the change quickly.

## Expected Behaviour

The employee should:

1. Pause before making any change.
2. Check whether the request follows normal process.
3. Verify the bank detail change using trusted supplier contact details already held on file.
4. Avoid using phone numbers or email addresses provided in the suspicious request.
5. Follow approval and segregation of duties requirements.
6. Escalate suspicious pressure or unusual instructions.
7. Record verification steps.
8. Report suspected fraud to finance leadership and security.

## Key Warning Signs

Employees should look out for:

- Urgency or pressure
- New bank details
- Slight changes in supplier email address
- Requests to bypass normal process
- Unusual tone or timing
- Confidentiality demands
- Requests from senior leaders outside normal workflow
- Attachments or links claiming to confirm bank changes
- Phone calls reinforcing the email request
- Unusual payment destination or country

## AI-Era Considerations

Attackers may use AI to make fraud attempts more convincing.

AI-enabled fraud may include:

- Polished supplier emails with no spelling errors
- Voice-cloned calls from fake executives or suppliers
- Deepfake video calls
- Personalized messages based on public information
- Fake invoices generated to match supplier style
- Multi-channel attacks combining email, phone, and messaging apps

Employees should not rely on voice, writing style, or apparent professionalism as proof of authenticity.

## Discussion Questions

Use these questions during the exercise:

1. What makes this request suspicious?
2. Who should verify the bank detail change?
3. What contact details should be used for verification?
4. Should the payment be paused?
5. Who should be informed?
6. What evidence should be recorded?
7. What if the request appears to come from a senior executive?
8. What if the caller sounds like a known supplier contact?
9. What if the payment deadline is today?
10. How should this be reported?

## Verification Protocol

For supplier bank detail changes:

1. Do not rely on the email request alone.
2. Use trusted supplier contact details already held in the system.
3. Call or verify through an approved supplier portal.
4. Require dual approval where policy requires it.
5. Record who verified the change and when.
6. Do not use contact details supplied in the suspicious email.
7. Escalate if there is pressure to bypass the process.

## Exercise Injects

Use these optional injects to make the scenario more realistic.

### Inject 1: Urgent Follow-Up

The supplier sends another email saying payment must be made today to avoid service disruption.

Discussion:

- Does urgency change the verification requirement?
- Who approves urgent payments?
- Should service disruption threats be escalated?

### Inject 2: Executive Pressure

A message appears to come from a senior leader asking finance to process the payment quickly.

Discussion:

- How should executive instructions be verified?
- Should seniority override process?
- What is the escalation route?

### Inject 3: Voice-Cloned Call

An employee receives a call that sounds like a known supplier contact confirming the bank change.

Discussion:

- Is voice enough to confirm identity?
- What second channel should be used?
- Should this be treated as suspicious?

### Inject 4: Payment Already Made

The payment has already been processed before the fraud is identified.

Discussion:

- Who must be notified?
- Can the bank be contacted?
- Should legal, insurance, or regulators be involved?
- What evidence should be preserved?
- What lessons should be shared?

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|

| IT Team | Review email headers, account activity, and technical indicators |
| Legal / Compliance | Advise on reporting obligations |
| Senior Leadership | Support process compliance and risk decisions |
| Managers | Reinforce verification and escalation expectations |

## Follow-Up Awareness Message

Use a message like this:

> Always verify payment changes through trusted contact details already held by the organization. Do not rely on email instructions, phone numbers in the request, or urgent pressure. If a payment request feels unusual, pause and escalate.

## Success Criteria

This exercise is successful when:

- Finance staff understand verification requirements.
- Urgent requests do not bypass controls.
- Staff know how to escalate suspicious payment requests.
- AI-enabled fraud risks are understood.
- Lessons learned are documented.
- Payment processes are strengthened.

## Disclaimer

This scenario should be adapted to the organization’s finance process, approval workflow, supplier management controls, fraud response procedures, and legal obligations.
