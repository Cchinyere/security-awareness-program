# Lost Device Scenario

## Purpose

This exercise helps organizations test whether employees know what to do when a work device is lost, stolen, or left unattended.

Lost devices can expose sensitive data, create unauthorized access risk, and lead to regulatory or reputational consequences if not reported quickly.

## Objectives

The objectives are to:

1. Reinforce the importance of quick lost device reporting.
2. Test whether employees know who to contact.
3. Confirm whether the organization can disable access or wipe devices where appropriate.
4. Improve employee understanding of device security.
5. Identify gaps in asset management, endpoint security, and reporting processes.

## Target Audience

This exercise is suitable for:

- All staff
- Remote workers
- Hybrid workers
- Mobile workers
- Executives
- Contractors
- Employees who travel for work
- Teams handling sensitive data

## Scenario Overview

An employee loses a work laptop while travelling home from a meeting. The laptop may contain access to email, shared files, business applications, and locally stored documents.

The employee is unsure whether to report it immediately or wait to see if the device is found.

## Expected Behaviour

Employees should:

1. Report the lost device immediately.
2. Contact IT or the service desk using the approved reporting route.
3. Provide details of when and where the device was lost.
4. Confirm whether any sensitive data may be stored on the device.
5. Avoid trying to hide the loss out of embarrassment.
6. Follow instructions from IT and security.
7. Change passwords if instructed.
8. Cooperate with any follow-up investigation.

## Key Learning Points

Employees should understand that:

- Quick reporting reduces risk.
- Lost devices can create data breach and account compromise risks.
- Encryption and remote wipe controls only help if the organization knows the device is missing.
- Staff should not delay reporting because they are embarrassed.
- Physical security is part of cybersecurity.

## Discussion Questions

Use these questions during a team discussion or tabletop exercise:

1. Who should the employee contact first?
2. What information should be provided in the report?
3. What should IT do after receiving the report?
4. Should the device be remotely locked or wiped?
5. Should the employee’s account access be reviewed?
6. Could sensitive data have been stored locally?
7. Does the incident need to be escalated to privacy or compliance?
8. What if the device belongs to a senior executive?
9. What if the device is found later?
10. What awareness message should be shared after the incident?

## AI-Era Considerations

Lost devices may create additional risks where employees have access to AI tools or sensitive prompts.

Consider:

- Was the employee logged into approved or unapproved AI tools?
- Could confidential prompts or outputs be accessible?
- Was sensitive data stored in browser sessions?
- Were credentials, API keys, or tokens stored on the device?
- Could the device provide access to AI-enabled business systems?
- Should AI tool sessions be revoked?

## Preventive Controls

Organizations should consider:

- Full disk encryption
- Strong authentication
- MFA
- Device lock policies
- Remote wipe capability
- Asset management
- Endpoint detection and response
- Session timeout
- Conditional access
- Mobile device management
- Clear lost device reporting process
- Regular awareness reminders

## Scenario Variations

You can adapt the exercise using these variations:

### Variation 1: Lost Laptop on Public Transport

An employee leaves a laptop on a train.

Focus areas:

- Immediate reporting
- Remote lock or wipe
- Data exposure assessment
- Account session review

### Variation 2: Stolen Mobile Phone

An employee’s work phone is stolen from a café.

Focus areas:

- MFA prompts
- Email access
- Mobile device management
- Password reset

### Variation 3: Lost USB Drive

An employee loses a USB drive containing work documents.

Focus areas:

- Data classification
- Encryption
- Removable media policy
- Privacy escalation

### Variation 4: Executive Lost Device

A senior executive loses a laptop while travelling.

Focus areas:

- High-value data
- Sensitive communications
- Board or financial documents
- Reputational impact

## Metrics

Track:

| Metric | Description |
|---|---|
| Time to Report | Time between loss and reporting |
| Time to Lock or Wipe | Time taken to secure the device |
| Devices Encrypted | Whether lost devices were encrypted |
| Sensitive Data Exposure | Whether sensitive data was at risk |
| Repeat Incidents | Repeat lost device events by team or location |
| Training Completion | Completion of device security awareness |

## Follow-Up Awareness Message

Use a message like this:

> If a work device is lost or stolen, report it immediately. Do not wait to see if it turns up. Early reporting helps IT and security protect accounts, data, and systems. You will not be blamed for reporting quickly.

## Success Criteria

This exercise is successful when:

- Employees know how to report lost devices.
- Reporting happens quickly.
- IT and security actions are clear.
- Device protection controls are understood.
- Data exposure risks are assessed.
- Lessons learned are captured.

## Disclaimer

This scenario should be adapted to the organization’s device policies, asset management process, privacy obligations, and technical controls.
