# Incident Response Tabletop Exercise

## Purpose

This tabletop exercise helps organizations test how well teams understand their responsibilities during a cybersecurity incident.

The exercise is discussion-based. It is designed to test decision-making, escalation, communication, coordination, and awareness of incident response procedures.

## Objectives

The objectives are to:

1. Test incident response roles and responsibilities.
2. Improve decision-making during a security incident.
3. Confirm escalation routes.
4. Identify gaps in communication and coordination.
5. Improve understanding of legal, regulatory, operational, and reputational considerations.
6. Capture lessons learned for improvement.

## Target Audience

This exercise may involve:

- Security team
- IT operations
- Service desk
- GRC or compliance
- Legal or data protection
- HR
- Communications team
- Business continuity team
- Senior leadership
- Department managers
- Third-party suppliers where relevant

## Scenario Overview

The organization receives multiple reports of suspicious emails. Shortly after, several users report that they cannot access files. The IT team identifies unusual activity on a shared file server or cloud storage platform.

There is concern that the organization may be experiencing a ransomware or account compromise incident.

## Exercise Format

| Item | Description |
|---|---|
| Exercise Type | Discussion-based tabletop |
| Duration | 60 to 120 minutes |
| Delivery Method | In person or online |
| Facilitator | Security lead, GRC lead, or incident response coordinator |
| Participants | Cross-functional incident stakeholders |
| Output | Lessons learned and improvement action plan |

## Pre-Exercise Preparation

Before the exercise:

1. Confirm the scenario.
2. Identify participants.
3. Prepare injects and discussion questions.
4. Confirm the incident response process.
5. Prepare an attendance list.
6. Assign a note taker.
7. Define the expected outputs.
8. Agree confidentiality expectations.

## Scenario Timeline

### Stage 1: Initial Alert

Several employees report suspicious emails with links to what appears to be a file-sharing platform.

Discussion questions:

- Who receives these reports?
- How are suspicious emails triaged?
- What information should be collected?
- Who decides whether this is a security incident?
- What immediate advice should be given to staff?

### Stage 2: Suspicious Account Activity

The security team detects unusual login activity from a user account.

Discussion questions:

- Who investigates the account activity?
- What logs should be reviewed?
- Should the account be disabled?
- How is the user contacted?
- Is there a need to check for wider compromise?

### Stage 3: Business Impact

A department reports that files are unavailable or appear to have been changed.

Discussion questions:

- How is the business impact assessed?
- Who declares a major incident?
- Which systems or teams are affected?
- What are the containment options?
- Are backups available and tested?

### Stage 4: Communication

Staff begin asking whether it is safe to use email and shared drives.

Discussion questions:

- Who approves internal communications?
- What should be communicated to staff?
- Who communicates with customers or partners if needed?
- When should leadership be informed?
- Are regulators or external parties involved?

### Stage 5: Recovery and Lessons Learned

The incident is contained and recovery begins.

Discussion questions:

- Who approves system restoration?
- How is evidence preserved?
- How are lessons learned captured?
- What awareness messages should be shared after the incident?
- What controls should be improved?

## AI-Era Considerations

The tabletop should consider how AI may affect the incident.

Possible AI-related discussion points:

- Was the phishing email AI-generated?
- Did attackers use deepfake or voice cloning to support the attack?
- Were employees targeted with personalized social engineering?
- Did anyone paste incident details into an unapproved AI tool?
- Could AI tools assist with triage, and what human review is required?
- Are AI-generated outputs trusted too quickly during incident analysis?

## Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility During Exercise |
|---|---|
| Security Team | Investigates, contains, and advises on cyber risk |
| IT Team | Supports system containment, recovery, and technical remediation |
| Service Desk | Handles user reports and escalates suspicious activity |
| GRC / Compliance | Advises on policy, risk, and control implications |
| Legal / Privacy | Advises on legal and data protection obligations |
| Communications | Supports internal and external messaging |
| HR | Supports employee-related concerns |
| Leadership | Supports decision-making, prioritization, and resource allocation |
| Business Teams | Provide business impact information |

## Key Decisions to Test

The exercise should test whether the organization can decide:

- When an event becomes an incident
- Who owns the response
- When to disable accounts
- When to isolate devices or systems
- When to inform senior leadership
- When to involve legal, privacy, or regulators
- Whether to communicate externally
- When systems can be restored
- How lessons learned will be tracked

## Evidence to Capture

During the exercise, capture:

- Attendees
- Key decisions
- Gaps identified
- Confusion around roles
- Missing contact details
- Communication issues
- Process weaknesses
- Control weaknesses
- Training needs
- Improvement actions

## Success Criteria

The exercise is successful when:

- Participants understand their roles.
- Escalation routes are clear.
- Key decisions are discussed.
- Communication gaps are identified.
- Lessons learned are documented.
- Improvement actions are assigned.
- Awareness needs are identified.

## Follow-Up Actions

After the exercise:

1. Share a summary of lessons learned.
2. Assign owners to improvement actions.
3. Update incident response procedures if required.
4. Update awareness materials based on gaps.
5. Brief leadership on key risks.
6. Schedule a follow-up review.

## Disclaimer

This tabletop exercise should be adapted to the organization’s incident response plan, business processes, legal requirements, systems, suppliers, and risk profile.
